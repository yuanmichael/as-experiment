What the AS receptor actually touches

Four names keep coming up around CASSVGLYSTDTQYF — TRAV21, TRBV9, TRBJ2-3, and the (Y/F)ST core. Each is a different physical part of the same object. Here is where each one sits, and which of them our sequencing can see.

One receptor, six loops, two surfaces

A T cell receptor is two protein chains, alpha and beta. Each chain ends in three flexible loops called CDR1, CDR2 and CDR3 — six in total — and those loops are the only parts that touch anything. What they touch is a short peptide lying in a groove on the HLA molecule of another cell.

Where the letters in the sequence come from

Our CDR3β string is not one thing. Its front end is copied from the V gene, its back end from the J gene, and only the few letters in between are improvised when the chromosome is cut and rejoined. The one letter that touches the peptide sits right at the join.

The family, and what we have observed

Hanson 2020 defines the motif as CASSVG(V/I/L)(Y/F)STDTQYF — six sequences, generated by two choices at residues 97 and 98. Counts below are presence at two or more reads in the Komech cohort after thinning every subject to 800,000 junction reads.

Two further sequences sit next to this family and should be kept in a separate column. CASSVATYSTDTQYF is Yang's AS4.3 — a different junction, but a receptor they solved a structure for — at 4 AS, 0 healthy here. CASSLGLFSTDTQYF and CASSLGLYSTDTQYF are one edit from family members at 7 and 4 AS carriers with 0 controls, but we found them, so they are exploratory rather than pre-specified.

Do they actually look alike? Yes — to within half an ångström

Yang 2022 solved seven crystal structures of these receptors bound to HLA-B27. Three of them are family members holding the same peptide, PRPF3, which makes the comparison clean: same groove, same antigen, different CDR3β sequence. Which half of the receptor our data contains

Every bulk repertoire dataset in this project sequences the beta chain only. The alpha chain — and therefore CDR1α, the loop that reaches peptide position 4 — has never been in any file we hold.
